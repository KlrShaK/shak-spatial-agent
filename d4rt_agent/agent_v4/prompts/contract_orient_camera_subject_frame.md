### How to read this measurement

This is which side of the subject the observer was on, in the subject's own
terms — its front, back, left or right — at three moments: the first frame both
a position and an unambiguous facing existed, the middle, and the last.

**The sides are the subject's, not yours.** "The subject's left" is the
subject's own left as it faces, which is on the opposite side of the image from
your left when the subject faces the camera. The bearing given alongside each
label makes this explicit:

- `0` — the observer is directly in **front** of the subject (it is facing the camera)
- `+90` — the observer is off the subject's **right**
- `+180` or `-180` — the observer is **behind** the subject
- `-90` — the observer is off the subject's **left**

Quadrants are 90 degrees wide and centred on those four values.

**Answer with the transition.** These questions are shaped "from the subject's X
to its Y". Use the first and last labels; the midpoint shows you which way it
travelled between them, which matters when the two ends are on opposite sides
and more than one route could join them.

**Boundary margins.** Each label comes with how many degrees it sits from the
nearest boundary. A label 2 degrees from a boundary is one small error away from
its neighbour — if the report flags a checkpoint as not robust, weigh the
neighbouring sector as a real possibility and say so in your limitations rather
than asserting the label.

An octant label (`front-right`, `back-left`, …) is given beside each quadrant for
exactly this reason: it tells you which neighbour the observer is leaning toward.
