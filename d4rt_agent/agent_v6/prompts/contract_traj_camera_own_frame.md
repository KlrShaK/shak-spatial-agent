Forward, left and right use the camera's starting heading. Up and down follow gravity when a levelled frame is available; otherwise they use the camera's starting axes, as stated in the report. A tilted, unlevelled camera can therefore split vertical movement across down and forward.

The table lists chronological phases. Read translation together with rotation: sideways motion without pan is a sideways move; pan without translation is turning on the spot. Pan is positive to the camera's right, tilt is positive upward, and roll is positive when the camera's right side dips. Angles are unwrapped, so complete turns survive.

For an orbit seen from above, translation LEFT with pan RIGHT is CLOCKWISE; translation RIGHT with pan LEFT is COUNTER-CLOCKWISE. The pan compensates for the sideways translation to keep the scene in view. Use these measured signs even when the scene appears to sweep in the opposite direction on screen.

Translation and each rotation have separate noise floors. A change at or below its floor is not measured motion. A small net displacement does not imply stillness when the phases show an out-and-back path. Reported units and measurement spans apply only to the quantities beside them.
