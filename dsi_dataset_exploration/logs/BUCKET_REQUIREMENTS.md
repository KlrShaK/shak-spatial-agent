# Input/output requirements for the 4 confirmed buckets

Hand analysis, not generated by `taxonomy_report.py` — kept as a separate file so a
future `taxonomy_report.py` rerun doesn't overwrite it. Cross-references
`d4rt_agent/sam_orientany_d4rt_test/testing_splines.ipynb` (the current
measurement prototype) and `d4rt_agent/prompts/dsi_bench_system.md` (the
existing DSI-Bench system prompt's "Question → measurement" table).

## 1. subject_own_frame (cate 0 + cate 1)

**Understanding needed:** how the subject moved through the scene, described in
a frame anchored to the subject itself (its own starting position + facing),
independent of whatever the camera was doing.

**Inputs**
- The subject's 3D position, tracked across the frames it's visible.
- The subject's own forward axis (from two named points — front/rear — or an
  orientation estimator) to build the local frame the trajectory is projected
  into.
- If the camera also moves (cate 1): the subject's position read from **one
  fixed camera viewpoint** across frames, so camera egomotion cancels by
  construction rather than contaminating the reading.

**Outputs**
- Displacement decomposed along the subject's own forward / lateral / vertical
  axes.
- Sign and magnitude of each component against a noise floor, to distinguish
  "moving forward" from "basically unchanged" from a diagonal/turn combo.
- Enough of the trajectory's shape (not just endpoints) to tell a straight
  path from a turn or an orbit.

**Prototype coverage:** the notebook's `subject own frame` bucket projects the
trajectory into the initial usable OAV2 subject basis and reports both natural
language and segment metrics.

## 2. camera_own_frame (cate 2 + cate 3)

**Understanding needed:** symmetric to (1), but for the observer — how the
camera moved, relative to where it started and which way it was facing.

**Inputs**
- ≥3 well-separated points that are reliably static in the scene, tracked
  across frames — camera pose isn't reported directly, so egomotion has to be
  read off the apparent (inverted) motion of the static background.
- In cate 3 (dynamic scene): a way to keep static background separate from
  moving foreground objects, so the recovered camera motion isn't
  contaminated by scene motion.
- The camera's own starting orientation, to express the recovery as
  forward/lateral/vertical translation plus pan/tilt/roll rotation (needed to
  tell "moved" from "just turned" from "orbited").

**Outputs**
- Camera translation along its own axes.
- Camera rotation (yaw/pitch/roll) over the same span.
- A reliability signal — this is inferred indirectly from sparse points, so
  noisier than directly tracking a target.

**Prototype coverage:** the notebook's `camera own frame` bucket reports the
recovered camera-centre trajectory in the frame-0 OpenCV camera basis.

## 3. distance_change (cate 4)

**Understanding needed:** a single scalar — how far apart the subject and
observer are at the start vs. the end of the window the subject is actually
visible in. The simplest of the four; no directional/frame reasoning at all.

**Inputs**
- The subject's 3D position at the earliest and latest frames it's actually
  visible, each read in its own camera's frame at that same timestamp (no
  common basis needed — range is frame-invariant, so norms from different
  viewpoints are directly comparable).

**Outputs**
- Range at those two points, their signed delta, and that delta measured
  against the noise floor — enough to say closer / farther / unchanged /
  cannot-be-determined.

**Prototype coverage:** the notebook's `distance change` bucket reports
start/mid/end natural language plus per-frame range metrics and a noise floor.

## 4. camera_subject_frame (cate 5)

**Understanding needed:** where the camera sits around the subject, described
not as a distance but as a sector of the subject's own body (front/back/
left/right), and how that sector changes over the clip.

**Inputs**
- The subject's own forward axis at the frames in question (orientation
  estimation, or two named points) — defines the sector boundaries.
- The subject's 3D position at those same frames, in each frame's own camera
  basis (equivalently, the camera-to-subject vector, since the camera is that
  basis's origin) — tells you which side of the subject the camera is
  actually on.

**Outputs**
- The camera's angular position relative to the subject's own axes
  (front/back/left/right) at the start and at the end of the relevant window.
- The transition between those two sectors — the "from X's side to Y" answer
  DSI-Bench expects.

**Prototype coverage:** the notebook's `camera relative to subject` bucket
projects the subject-to-camera vector into the per-frame OAV2 basis, quantizes
the bearing into eight 45-degree sectors, and reports start/mid/end prose plus
bearing and component metrics.
