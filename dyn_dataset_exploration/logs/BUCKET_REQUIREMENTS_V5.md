# Input/output requirements for the Dyn-Bench measurement buckets

Hand analysis, not generated by `taxonomy_report.py` — kept as a separate file so a
future `taxonomy_report.py` rerun doesn't overwrite it. The Dyn-Bench counterpart of
`dsi_dataset_exploration/logs/BUCKET_REQUIREMENTS.md`, and written in the same form:
*Understanding needed / Inputs / Outputs / Where AgentV4 stands*.

Counts are from `TAXONOMY_REPORT.md`: `n` is out of the 600-question stratified sample,
`est.` is the post-stratified estimate over all 7000 VQA rows.

**What carries over.** Of AgentV4's four buckets, three survive Dyn-Bench essentially
unchanged — `traj_camera_own_frame` (18, est. 606), `sub_obs_distance_change` (47, est.
593) and `orient_camera_subject_frame` (25, est. 228). Their specs are unchanged from the
DSI-Bench document and are not restated here.

**What does not.** `traj_subject_own_frame` — AgentV4's flagship bucket, the one both
DSI-Bench `cate` 0 and 1 mapped onto — collects **10 of 600** questions here (est. 83, or
1%). Dyn-Bench almost never asks where an object went *in its own axes*. It asks where it
went relative to the camera, relative to the scene, or relative to another object. That
single substitution is the largest structural difference between the two benchmarks, and
sections 1–3 below are its three replacements.

---

## 1. `traj_subject_scene_frame` — n=53, est. 773 (11%)

> *"Between 0.0s and 2.17s, how does the person's position relative to the snowy slope change?"*
> *"What is the overall motion pattern of the green truck relative to the road's curvature?"*

**Understanding needed:** where an object went through the scene, described against
something in the scene that is not moving — a slope, a road, a doorway, the room — rather
than against the object's own facing or the camera's.

**Inputs**
- The subject's 3D position across the frames it is visible, as `traj3d_query.query_subject`
  already produces.
- A **static-scene reference frame**. AgentV4 recovers camera pose from the 12×12 grid in
  `traj3d_analyze.recover_camera_poses`; composing the subject track with the inverse of
  that pose already yields a world-frame track. What is missing is the *anchor*: the
  question names a landmark ("the slope", "the front door"), and that landmark has to be
  grounded and localised, not just implied.
- Therefore a second grounding pass: SAM3 on the landmark phrase, and a D4RT query on its
  points — even when the landmark is static, because its 3D position is what defines the
  frame's origin.

**Outputs**
- Displacement of the subject in world axes, plus the same displacement expressed as a
  bearing and range against the landmark.
- Whether the subject approached, receded, passed, or circled the landmark, against a
  noise floor as `traj3d_buckets.segment_report` already computes one.
- The camera-pose reliability signal, because a bad pose solve corrupts the world frame
  silently — `camera_median_relative_rmse` is already carried in every bucket report's
  `pipeline` block.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** every ingredient exists; nothing assembles them. `level_basis`
in `traj3d_buckets` already builds a gravity-aligned frame from `estimate_world_up` — but
its heading is deliberately still the camera's frame-0 facing, flattened, not anything in
the scene. So the vertical axis is already a world axis and the horizontal one is not. The
gap is the second grounding, and re-anchoring the heading on the named landmark.

## 2. `subject_subject_relation` — n=56, est. 864 (12%)

> *"Do the two people get closer or farther apart during the video?"*
> *"Between the start and the end, how does the person's position relative to the lamp change?"*

**Understanding needed:** the distance and bearing between **two scene objects**, neither
of which is the camera, and how that changes.

**Inputs**
- Two independent subject trajectories in one common frame. Since both are queried from
  the same D4RT encoding of the same clip, they already share a frame at each timestamp —
  no registration step is needed, which is the one piece of good news here.
- Both objects' visibility masks, because "closer or farther" is only defined over the
  frames where both are visible. `sub_obs_distance_change` already restricts to a
  visible window; this needs the intersection of two.

**Outputs**
- Inter-object range at start, middle and end of the jointly-visible window, its signed
  delta, and that delta against a noise floor — structurally the same `checkpoints`
  metrics shape `sub_obs_distance_change` already emits.
- Bearing of object B in object A's frame, when the question is directional
  ("moves past", "moves to the left of") rather than purely radial.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** blocked at the orchestration layer, not the geometry layer.
`agent_v4/contracts.py` has exactly one `name_subject` action, `agent_v4/orchestrator.py`
threads exactly one subject through its fixed sequence, and `agent_v4/pipeline.py` caches
per `(video, subject)` — that cache is already the right shape for two entries, it is the
state machine above it that only ever asks for one. See `AGENT_V5_GAPS.md` §1.

## 3. `traj_subject_camera_frame` — n=30, est. 183 (3%)

> *"Between the start (0.0s) and the end (1.63s), how does the kitesurfer's position relative to the camera change?"*

**Understanding needed:** where the object went as the camera sees it — nearer/farther,
left/right, up/down in the camera's axes — not in the object's own axes.

This is `subject_observer_frame`, one of the nine buckets the DSI-Bench pass defined and
then found **absent** from DSI-Bench. It is present here. It is also the bucket the first
classification pass kept filing under `OTHER` with rationales like *"asks for the object's
displacement in the camera frame, which is not represented by any single listed bucket"* —
which is how it got added.

**Inputs**
- The subject's 3D position per frame, read in **that frame's own camera basis** — which is
  exactly what D4RT returns before any of AgentV4's frame conversions are applied. Strictly
  less work than `traj_subject_own_frame`, which needs orientation on top.
- No orientation estimate at all, and no camera-pose recovery. This is the cheapest of the
  three replacements.

**Outputs**
- Per-axis displacement in the OpenCV camera frame (+x right, +y down, +z forward), split
  into a depth component and a lateral one, since the answer options separate them
  ("moves closer and to the left").
- A noise floor per axis, so "moved left" is distinguishable from "stayed put".

**Where AgentV4 stands:** the data is already on disk in the right frame. This is a report
builder and a contract file, and close to nothing else — the highest ratio of coverage to
effort in this document.

---

## 4. `camera_motion_profile` — n=41, est. 650 (9%)

> *"What is the movement speed (movement rhythm) of the camera?"* · *"Is there noticeable camera shake in the video?"*

**Understanding needed:** not *where* the camera went but *how* — steady vs. accelerating
vs. stop-start, smooth vs. handheld-shaky.

**Inputs**
- The recovered camera centre per frame, which `traj3d_analyze.recover_camera_poses`
  already writes to `analysis/camera_poses.npz`.
- **The raw track, not only the smoothed one.** Shake *is* the residual that
  `traj3d_buckets.running_mean` removes. `displacement_noise` already computes exactly this
  residual as a noise floor — this bucket promotes that discarded quantity to the answer.
- Frame timestamps, to turn per-frame displacement into a speed rather than a per-step
  distance.

**Outputs**
- Speed as a function of time, summarised enough to separate "consistent and smooth",
  "stable then a sudden fast swing", and "moving then stopped" — the three distractors
  Dyn-Bench actually offers.
- A jitter statistic: magnitude of the high-frequency residual relative to the net path
  length, with a threshold calibrated on clips known to be tripod-mounted.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** the numbers exist and are thrown away. `angle_noise_deg` and
`displacement_noise` in `traj3d_buckets.py` compute the residual specifically in order to
*suppress* it.

---

## 5. `subject_motion_profile` — n=38, est. 424 (6%)

> *"Does the large passenger jet remain stationary or move during the video?"*
> *"Does the person's movement across the court occur continuously or in short bursts?"*

The subject-side mirror of §4: does it move at all, and is the motion continuous,
intermittent, or one brief burst.

**Inputs:** the aggregated subject trajectory plus its per-frame visibility; timestamps.

**Outputs:** per-frame speed against the trajectory's own noise floor, reduced to a
moving/not-moving run-length encoding — the count and duration of motion runs is the
answer, not the total displacement.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** `traj3d_buckets.segment_report` already produces
`N_LANGUAGE_SEGMENTS = 10` uniform-duration segments with `direction`, `distance` and
`above_noise` per segment. The run-length encoding is a reduction over exactly that table.
`uniform_time_segments` already takes `n_segments` as a parameter; nothing above it ever
passes a different value, and ten segments over a 32-frame window is likely too coarse to
separate "short bursts" from "continuous". Exposing the existing parameter is the change,
not adding one.

---

## 6. `subject_visibility` — n=35, est. 321 (5%)

> *"At the end of the video, is the red fox still in the frame?"*
> *"In which area of the frame is the main object located for the majority of the time?"*

**Inputs:** the D4RT visibility logits already stored in `d4rt/subject.npz`, plus the SAM3
mask sequence in `masks/masks.npz`; and for the "which area" variant, the mask centroid in
normalised image coordinates.

**Outputs:** present/absent per frame; the frames where that flips; and a dwell histogram
over a coarse image-plane grid.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** the inputs are already computed and already thresholded —
`traj3d_analyze` applies `sigmoid()` and `VISIBILITY_THRESHOLD = 0.5` today. There is no
report that surfaces them. One caveat: `visibility` conflates "occluded" with "outside the
frame", and Dyn-Bench's options sometimes distinguish them; the mask's contact with the
image border disambiguates.

---

## 7. `camera_zoom_vs_dolly` — n=22, est. 238 (3%)

> *"Was zooming (in or out) used during recording?"*

**Understanding needed:** separate a focal-length change from a camera translation along
its own optical axis. Both make the subject fill more of the frame; only one moves the
camera.

**Inputs:** per-frame intrinsics from the camera grid correspondences, and the recovered
camera centre over the same span.

**Outputs:** focal-length trend over the clip with a confidence, camera forward
translation over the same span, and the joint verdict (zoom / dolly / both / neither).

**Where AgentV4 stands:** closer than it looks. `traj3d_analyze` already calls
`estimate_intrinsics(camera["xyz_3d"][0], grid_uv_norm, image_hw)` — and because
`query_camera` varies `t_cam` over all 32 frames while holding `t_src = t_tgt = 0`, that
array is one instant seen from 32 cameras. Indexing `[t]` instead of `[0]` in a loop gives
a per-frame focal length from code that already exists; a zoom is a trend in that series.

Two caveats before trusting it. `estimate_intrinsics`'s own docstring says the focal
length "is only used for the off-axis orientation correction, where a few percent of error
is immaterial" — this bucket would be the first consumer that needs it to be accurate, so
it needs its own validation. And the conditioning warning in `traj3d_analyze` (the
yellow-jacket clip returned fx=51 against a true ~256 from subject points) means the grid,
not the subject, must be the input at every frame, not just the first.

## 8. `apparent_size_change` — n=21, est. 190 (3%)

> *"Visually, does the main object get larger or smaller over time?"* (options name zoom and physical motion as combined causes)

**Inputs:** the SAM3 mask area per frame, normalised by image area. Nothing 3D.

**Outputs:** the normalised-area trend, with a monotone/fluctuating verdict — the fourth
option is always "fluctuating", so a single endpoint delta is not enough.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** the masks are on disk; no report reads their area. This is the
cheapest bucket in the document and needs no D4RT query at all, which also makes it the
natural fallback when the pipeline declines.

---

## 9. `camera_elevation_rel_subject` — n=13, est. 227 (3%)

> *"What is the camera angle relative to the person in the pink snowsuit?"* (high / eye-level / low)

The elevation component of the same subject↔camera bearing that
`orient_camera_subject_frame` already computes for azimuth.

**Inputs:** identical to `orient_camera_subject_frame` — the subject's facing from
Orient-Anything-V2 and the camera-to-subject vector — plus the gravity direction from
`estimate_world_up`, since "high angle" is defined against gravity, not against the
subject's own up axis.

**Outputs:** elevation angle at start/middle/end, quantised into above / level / below with
a band wide enough to absorb OAV2's elevation error.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** `orient_camera_subject_frame` quantises the bearing into eight
45° azimuth sectors and discards elevation. This is an extra field on an existing report,
not a new pipeline path.

---

## 10. `event_order` — n=36, est. 234 (3%)

> *"Which happens first: the camera begins a noticeable rightward pan, or the person in the dark jacket enters the scene?"*

**Understanding needed:** localise two named events in time and compare their onsets. Note
that the two events are routinely of *different kinds* — one camera-side, one object-side —
so this is a comparison across two other buckets' outputs, not a measurement of its own.

**Inputs:** whichever per-frame signal each named event is defined over (camera angular
velocity for "begins a pan"; visibility onset for "enters the scene"; subject speed for
"begins to move"), plus timestamps.

**Outputs:** an onset frame per event with a confidence, and the signed difference.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** nothing. It also does not fit the existing one-bucket-per-question
design: the host would have to run two measurements and compare them. This is the bucket
that most argues for AgentV5 letting a question request more than one measurement.

---

## 11. `scene_revisit` — n=28, est. 164 (2%)

> *"Is the background scene the same at the start and end of the video?"*

**Inputs:** appearance descriptors of the background (mask-complement) at the first and
last frames; optionally the recovered camera pose, since a large net rotation predicts a
different view.

**Outputs:** a same/different verdict with a similarity score.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** nothing, and unlike the others it is not a geometry problem — it
is image retrieval. A frame-pair similarity is enough; this does not need D4RT.

---

## 12. `metric_distance` — n=17, est. 225 (3%) · `metric_speed` — n=17, est. 203 (3%)

> *"At the 5-second mark, what is the approximate distance between the person and the small animal?"* → `A. 5 meters …`
> *"What is the approximate average speed of the gray SUV?"* → `A. 0.5 m/s …`

**Understanding needed:** a number in real-world units. Not an ordinal judgement.

**Inputs:** everything the ordinal buckets need, **plus an absolute scale**. D4RT
normalises each clip by its own mean depth (`traj3d_geometry.cloud_scale`), so every
distance AgentV4 reports is scale-relative and comparable only within one clip. There is no
route to metres from the current pipeline.

Three options, in ascending order of honesty and cost:
1. **Decline.** Return `available: False` with the reason, and let the agent answer from
   the frames. Correct, cheap, and gives up ~6% of the benchmark.
2. **Anchor on a known object.** Estimate scale from a recognised category with a tight
   size prior (an adult human ≈1.7 m, a sedan ≈4.5 m). Cheap, and wrong in a bounded way.
3. **Metric depth.** Add a metric monocular-depth model and fit the D4RT cloud to it.
   Correct in principle, a new model dependency, and a new failure mode.

**Outputs:** the quantity in metres or m/s, **with the scale source and its uncertainty
stated**, so the agent can decline to distinguish `10 m` from `15 m` when the scale prior
cannot support it. Dyn-Bench's metric options are usually spaced 2× apart, which option 2
can often resolve and option 1 cannot.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** structurally absent. This is a decision to make explicitly, not a
gap to close silently — see `AGENT_V5_GAPS.md` §3.

## 13. `instance_count` — n=18, est. 216 (3%)

> *"How many distinct people appear in the video?"*

**Inputs:** open-vocabulary detection of the named category on every sampled frame, and
association across frames so the same instance is not counted twice.

**Outputs:** the instance count, with per-instance first/last visible frame so a wrong
answer is diagnosable.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** `pipeline._anchor_candidates` already runs SAM3 over all frames
and keeps the top `MAX_CANDIDATES_SHOWN = 4` candidates — but for *disambiguation*, to help
the agent pick one. Counting needs the same detections kept and associated rather than
ranked and discarded, plus the cap lifted (Dyn-Bench's mask track has clips with up to 7
annotated objects).

---

## 14. `subject_orient_over_time` — n=17, est. 18 (0%)

> *"What happens to the kite surfer's orientation as the scene evolves?"*

Another of the nine DSI-Bench buckets that turned out absent there and present here.

**Inputs:** the OAV2 per-frame facing AgentV4 already computes, corrected for camera
rotation — which `traj3d_buckets.subject_heading_angles` already does.

**Outputs:** heading over time, its net change and its span, against `angle_noise_deg`.

**Where AgentV4 stands:** already computed and already used as an *intermediate* inside
`traj_subject_own_frame` and `orient_camera_subject_frame`. Reporting it directly is a
small new builder over existing arrays. Note the estimate (18 rows) is far below the
sample count (17) because the family this bucket concentrates in is the rarest one
sampled — this is a small bucket, cheap to add, and low priority on volume alone.

---

## 15. `semantic_only` — n=52, est. 495 (7%)

> *"What best describes the main activity in the video?"*

Not a measurement. It is the **decision not to measure**, and AgentV5 needs it as an
explicit route: AgentV4's `choose_bucket` has no such option, so today it picks one of four
geometric buckets and the host runs SAM3 + D4RT + OAV2 for a question about what a person
is doing. On the population estimate that is ~7% of all Dyn-Bench VQA rows spent on
pipeline work that cannot inform the answer.

**Outputs:** none. The contract is the interesting part — it should tell the agent to
answer from the frames and say that no measurement was taken, which is the wording
`agent_v4/orchestrator.py` already uses for its three `available: False` paths.

## Bucket-independent requirement: a time axis

`timestamped` questions cut across buckets 1, 2, 3, 10, 12 and 13 — 492 rows population-wide
name a second ("at 5.33 seconds", "between 0.0s and 2.17s"). Every bucket above whose
window is anything other than the whole clip needs:

- frames-to-seconds for the clip, derivable from `frame_sampling.json`'s `frame_indices`
  and `total_frames`, both already carried in `logs/index.json`;
- a way to evaluate a report over a **named sub-window** rather than the whole clip.
  `traj3d_buckets.uniform_time_segments` and `checkpoint_frames` both hard-code
  whole-clip spans today.

This is one change that unlocks part of six buckets, which makes it the highest-leverage
item in this document after the two-subject orchestrator.
